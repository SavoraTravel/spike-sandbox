# 2. The visitor

I watched it for a full minute before I moved. The light came on at a walking pace, not hurrying and not stopping, and it did not swing the way a lantern swings in a hand. Out there the wind was strong enough to lean on. I had seen it take a bucket off the step and put it in the sea. The light stayed level, a small yellow point with a little halo of rain around it, and it came on.

Bram had got up. He stood stiff-legged beside the stove with his head turned so that his good ear faced the window, and when I took my coat from the hook he came and pressed against my knee. He did not growl. He only leaned, which is what he does when he wants me to know where he is.

I took the big torch as well, though I didn't switch it on. The stairs down to the door are stone and I know them in the dark. At the bottom I stood with my hand on the bolt and listened, and heard the gale, and the sea working at the foot of the rock, and under both of those, nothing at all. No steps. I had expected steps.

Then someone knocked, three times, evenly spaced, as you would at a house in a town.

I had to clear my throat before I could use my voice. When I drew the bolt the wind took the door out of my hand and slammed it against the wall, and the rain came in across the flagstones. The person on the step was tall, in a long dark coat with the collar turned up, holding the light low at their side. It was a plain lamp with a glass chimney. The flame inside stood straight up and did not flicker.

I could not see a face. The hood or the collar or the dark took it. I noticed the coat hem, which hung heavy and straight, and the boots below it, which were dry. I noticed that, and then I made myself stop noticing it.

"The causeway's been shut since twenty to midnight," I said. It came out rough, like something taken from a drawer.

"I know," the visitor said. The voice was level and rather quiet, and I heard it clearly over the wind. "I saw that your lamp was out."

Behind me Bram made a small sound in his throat, the one he makes when he dreams. I stepped back from the door to let them in, and I could not afterwards have told you whether I had decided to.
