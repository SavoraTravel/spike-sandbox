# 1. The lamp

The lamp had been dark for three nights, and I had stopped climbing the stairs to look at it.

There is a particular silence to a lighthouse that does not light. The machinery still ticks as it cools, the glass still sings when the wind finds the gaps, but the beam that should sweep the water every twelve seconds simply isn't there. I kept counting to twelve anyway. Habit is what holds you together on a rock.

Bram did not care about the lamp. He cared about the stove, and about the corner of the blanket I let him have, and about his supper, which I gave him at seven as always. He ate with his good ear toward me, the way he does, in case I said something worth hearing. I didn't. I hadn't said anything worth hearing to anyone in eleven days, and the radio had been dead since the gale came in from the northwest and started throwing the rain sideways against the windows.

The part for the lamp was on the supply boat. The supply boat was four days away. I wrote that in the log, because the log is the only thing on Skerra Rock that listens.

Low tide came at twenty to ten. I watched the causeway rise out of the black water like a spine, glistening, and I watched it go under again two hours later, the way it always does. After that the rock is an island and nothing crosses.

I was banking the stove when Bram lifted his head.

He couldn't have heard anything. He hears nothing on his left side and very little on his right. But he was looking at the window, and when I followed his gaze I saw it: a small, steady light, the kind you hold in your hand, moving along the causeway toward us.

It was ten past midnight. The causeway had been underwater for half an hour.
